Start where you are

Get hands-on experience

You do not need a professional race-team internship before you can begin. Local racing, karting, student competitions, volunteering, and personal projects can all build useful experience.

01

Start as a fan

Go to a race

Attend a local karting or short-track event, or experience an IMSA, IndyCar, Formula 1, or NASCAR race. Watch how a weekend runs, learn the formats, and pay attention to preparation, pit stops, teamwork, and communication. You can start learning without driving or joining a team.

Find a club or watch party

Join a campus motorsports club or organize a race watch party to meet students with similar interests. Watching together is a low-pressure way to ask questions, compare observations, and find people for a karting trip, race visit, or student project.

02

Ways to get involved

Owner karting

Run, maintain, and set up a kart that you or your team owns. It gives you repeated practice with alignment, tire pressures, gearing, preparation, data, and driver feedback. Helping an existing owner or team is a useful first step before buying equipment.

Volunteer with a kart or local team

Individual kart owners, karting teams, and local racing teams may need a mechanic, data analyst, or pit-crew member. Contact teams before the event, explain what you can do, and offer reliable shop or race-day help.

Student race teams

Join a university team, school EV kart team, or motorsports club. Ask about design, build, testing, logistics, budgeting, and communications roles; you do not have to be the driver.

Internships

Look beyond race engineering: manufacturing, suppliers, simulation, electronics, PR, partnerships, finance, HR, and operations can all provide relevant experience. Show completed work and explain what you learned.

Rental karting

Practice driving without owning or maintaining a kart. Focus on consistent laps, racing lines, braking, and useful driver feedback. Local leagues can provide regular competition, though setup changes and data access depend on the venue.

Sim racing

Use repeatable practice and telemetry to study driving, compare laps, and prepare for competition. Collegiate leagues offer ways to race with other students. Simulation can build analysis and communication skills, but it does not replace real-world vehicle testing.

Local short tracks & ARCA

Look up local teams and ask about preparation, shop work, timing, equipment, or data support. ARCA and short-track teams differ in staffing and resources; useful help starts with understanding what a particular team needs.

Track days, autocross & event support

Use organized driving events to practice observation, data review, and setup documentation. Events may also need timing, scoring, registration, media, or marshal support, subject to their training and access rules.

03

What you can offer

Mechanic / shop helper

Help with cleaning, preparation, inspections, and maintenance under the team’s supervision. Be honest about your experience and learn its procedures.

Data analyst

Organize timing and telemetry, compare laps, record setup changes, and turn driver feedback into useful questions. A short, readable report is often more useful than a complicated dashboard.

Pit crew / race-day support

Help prepare tires and equipment, organize tools, track timing, and support communication or service allowed by the series. Training and credentials may be required.

Business, media & operations

Build a budget, organize travel and inventory, write a sponsor update, take useful photos, or help with social media. These responsibilities are part of running a racing team.

Start with a conversation.Visit a local kart club or race, meet owners and independent drivers, and ask what help they need. Learn the local classes and support options before buying a kart. Opportunities are by arrangement; volunteering does not guarantee unrestricted pit access.
