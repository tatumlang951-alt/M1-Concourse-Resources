Find your role

Careers in motorsports

Race teams need much more than engineers. Explore the work behind the car, from design and data to fabrication, logistics, partnerships, and people.

Job titles vary significantly between teams and series. A responsibility handled by a dedicated specialist on one team may be part of a broader role on another.

01

Engineering · Development

Aerodynamics Engineer

Develops aerodynamic performance through CFD, wind-tunnel testing, aero maps, geometry changes, and correlation.

Design Engineer

Designs components and assemblies around loads, packaging, manufacturing, reliability, and serviceability.

Metrology Engineer

Measures components and assemblies with tools such as CMMs and 3D scanners to verify geometry and tolerances.

R&D Engineer

Develops and validates new parts, materials, test rigs, methods, and performance concepts.

Manufacturing Engineer

Develops reliable processes for making and inspecting components.

02

Engineering · Competition

Data Engineer / Data Analyst

Processes telemetry and helps the engineers and driver understand what happened on track.

Engine / Powertrain Engineer

Supports performance, reliability, calibration, and analysis of the engine or powertrain.

Vehicle Dynamics & Simulation Engineer

Studies how tires, suspension, aero, loading, setup, and driver inputs affect the car. Builds and uses vehicle, tire, lap-time, and driver-in-the-loop models to compare setups, predict behavior, and correlate results with track data.

Tire Engineer

Studies pressure, temperature, wear, degradation, construction, and tire performance.

Systems Engineer

Supports electronics, sensors, data acquisition, communications, and applicable controls.

Performance Engineer

Combines telemetry, simulation, driver feedback, setup analysis, and performance tools.

Race Engineer

Works with the driver and engineering team to set up the car and make performance decisions during an event.

03

Race operations

Crew Chief

Leads the team’s technical and race decisions; authority and responsibilities depend on the series.

Car Chief

Coordinates the preparation, assembly, legality, and readiness of a particular car.

Team Manager / Operations

Organizes people, schedules, budgets, credentials, logistics, and event execution.

Pit Crew

Performs practiced race-service tasks such as tire changes, fueling where permitted, and adjustments.

Spotter

Communicates traffic, incidents, track conditions, and relevant observations to the driver.

Tire Specialist

Prepares, measures, tracks, and manages tires and their usage history.

04

Shop, fabrication & logistics

Mechanic / Race Technician

Builds, services, inspects, and troubleshoots the car before and during events.

Machinist / CNC Programmer

Makes precision components and fixtures, selecting machining strategies and checking dimensions.

Fabricator / Welder

Builds and repairs structures, brackets, tubing, and other fabricated components.

Composite Technician

Lays up, cures, bonds, repairs, and inspects carbon-fiber or other composite parts.

Engine / Gearbox Builder

Assembles and inspects powertrain components to documented specifications.

Truck Driver / Transporter Operator

Transports cars and equipment and often helps manage the transporter and paddock setup.

Parts & Inventory Coordinator

Tracks components, spares, consumables, service history, and purchasing needs.

05

Business, communications & people

Public Relations

Handles media relationships, interviews, announcements, and team communications.

Marketing

Plans campaigns and content that connect the team with its audience and partners.

Sponsorships / Partnerships

Finds partners, develops proposals, manages relationships, and delivers agreed benefits.

Accountant / Finance

Manages budgets, reporting, payments, payroll coordination, and financial planning.

Human Resources

Supports recruiting, onboarding, employee relations, benefits, and people processes.

IT / Technology Support

Keeps team computers, networks, accounts, storage, and communications working reliably at the shop and track. Supports troubleshooting, backups, cybersecurity, and the systems engineers use to share data.

Social Media / Content

Creates and publishes useful event, team, driver, and partner content.

Photographer / Videographer

Documents events and produces imagery for media, team, and sponsor use.

Graphic Designer

Creates visual materials for communications, presentations, merchandise, and liveries.

Hospitality / Guest Experience

Organizes sponsor and guest visits, event spaces, schedules, and service.

Sales / Merchandise

Manages retail products, customer service, and commercial opportunities.

06

Building a path into the industry

There is no single required route. Universities, trades, manufacturing, local racing, internships, and student teams can all lead to useful experience.

  1. Build foundational technical or business skills.
  2. Join a student racing or hands-on organization.
  3. Learn basic data analysis and telemetry.
  4. Complete small motorsports-related projects.
  5. Document your work and what you learned.
  6. Attend events and meet people working in racing.
  7. Apply for internships and realistic volunteer opportunities.
  8. Keep developing communication and teamwork.
