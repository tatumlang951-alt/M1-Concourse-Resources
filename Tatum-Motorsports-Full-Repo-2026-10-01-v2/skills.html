Build useful skills

Skills used in motorsports

Different jobs use different combinations of skills. Start with the fundamentals for the work you want to do, then develop them through a project or team.

01

Design & development

  • CADCreate parts and assemblies and check packaging before manufacturing.
  • Mechanical designChoose geometry, materials, fasteners, and load paths that fit the job.
  • GD&TUse geometric dimensioning and tolerancing to communicate how a part must fit and function.
  • ManufacturingUnderstand machining, forming, welding, and other production processes.
  • CompositesConsider fiber direction, layup, bonding, curing, and inspection.
  • FEAUse finite element analysis to study stress and deflection, with realistic loads and constraints.
  • CFDUse computational fluid dynamics to investigate airflow and cooling, then compare with testing.
  • MetrologyMeasure parts and assemblies and quantify whether they meet requirements.
  • QualityTrack inspection results, process variation, and the causes of defects.
  • 3D scanningCapture surface geometry for inspection, packaging, or comparison.
  • Reverse engineeringUnderstand an existing component through measurements and its function.
  • Design reviewsExplain assumptions and tradeoffs before committing to a design.
02

Data & software

  • ExcelBuild setup sheets, calculations, comparisons, and quick plots.
  • PythonClean data, automate repetitive tasks, and build analysis tools.
  • MATLABAnalyze signals, develop models, and run numerical studies.
  • Data visualizationMake plots that communicate a question and a usable conclusion.
  • Databases & SQLStore organized data and retrieve consistent comparisons.
  • Data structuresArrange data so it can be processed reliably and efficiently.
  • Telemetry analysisConnect measured channels to driver inputs and vehicle behavior.
  • AutomationMake repeatable workflows with clear checks and failure messages.
  • AI & machine learningUse appropriate models and assistants while checking inputs, leakage, and results.
  • Git / GitHubTrack changes, collaborate, and keep recoverable versions of your work.
  • Technical notesRecord channel definitions, units, assumptions, and decisions.
03

Simulation

  • Lap-time simulation (LTS)Estimate performance around a circuit and compare design or setup changes.
  • Vehicle modelingRepresent how the vehicle responds to forces, inputs, and conditions.
  • Tire modelingDescribe tire forces as load, slip, camber, and operating conditions change.
  • Driver-in-the-loop (DIL)Evaluate behavior with a human driver operating a simulator.
  • Static / quasi-static simulationStudy equilibrium states and steady operating conditions.
  • Offline simulationRun a modeled maneuver or lap without a live driver.
  • Design of experiments (DOE)Choose a useful set of tests to separate the influence of different inputs.
  • CorrelationCompare predictions with measured results and investigate disagreement.
  • Sensitivity studiesChange one input or a controlled set of inputs to see what matters most.
04

Trackside & testing

  • Sensors & instrumentationChoose sensors, mounting, wiring, ranges, and sampling rates.
  • Data acquisition (DAQ)Collect synchronized, usable measurements from the vehicle.
  • Sensor calibrationConvert raw outputs into physical values and check zero, scale, and drift.
  • Vehicle setupMeasure and document alignment, ride heights, corner weights, and settings.
  • Test planningDefine the question, procedure, conditions, and success criteria before running.
  • TroubleshootingUse evidence to isolate electrical, mechanical, or data problems.
  • Driver feedbackAsk clear questions and connect observations to specific corners and phases.
  • Race communicationGive concise, timely information and confirm important changes.
  • Test documentationKeep run sheets, conditions, changes, and conclusions together.
05

Professional skills

  • CommunicationExplain the conclusion and why it matters to the person using it.
  • Problem solvingDefine the problem, test assumptions, and work from evidence.
  • Technical writingMake a report understandable and repeatable by someone else.
  • PresentationShow the key result and answer questions without hiding uncertainty.
  • Project managementPlan time, budget, dependencies, and deliverables.
  • Team leadershipSet clear responsibilities, listen, and help people work together.
  • Working under pressurePrioritize the decision that matters now and use consistent procedures.
  • Engineering judgmentTurn data and experience into a decision while recognizing limitations.
